Yes, 2028 is a leap year. If you are asking is 2028 a leap year, the answer is yes. February 29, 2028 exists, and it falls on a Tuesday. It is 474 days after November 12, 2026, so the next leap day is still more than a year away. If you were born on February 29, your next real birthday is that Tuesday in 2028.

## Why February gets an extra day

Earth takes about 365.2422 days to travel once around the sun, not a neat 365. If the calendar ignored that extra quarter of a day, the seasons would drift: after a century, the calendar would be about 24 days ahead of the seasons, and July would slowly slide into spring weather. The leap day fixes the drift by adding one day, February 29, almost every four years. The century exceptions in the rules table keep the fix accurate over centuries, so the calendar stays lined up with the seasons for thousands of years. That is why 2028, like 2024 before it, gets a February 29.

## Leap years from 2024 to 2032

The three leap years in this stretch are easy to list, and each one adds a single extra day to February:

| Year | Leap year? | February 29 weekday |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | Yes | Thursday |
| 2025 | No | No February 29 |
| 2026 | No | No February 29 |
| 2027 | No | No February 29 |
| 2028 | Yes | Tuesday |
| 2029 | No | No February 29 |
| 2030 | No | No February 29 |
| 2031 | No | No February 29 |
| 2032 | Yes | Sunday |

The gap between leap days is four years here: February 29, 2024 to February 29, 2028 is 1,461 days, and February 29, 2028 to February 29, 2032 is also 1,461 days. Each four year stretch holds one leap day, which is why the count is 1,461 instead of 1,460.

## The three leap year rules

The full rules, with the century test and the history behind them, are in our guide [What Is a Leap Year?]({{ '/blog/what-is-a-leap-year/' | relative_url }}). The short version fits in one table:

| Rule | Test | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Divisible by 4 | Year divided by 4 leaves no remainder | 2028 divided by 4 is 507, so 2028 is a leap year |
| Century years are skipped | A year ending in 00 is not a leap year | 2100 is divisible by 4 but it is not a leap year |
| Unless divisible by 400 | A century year divisible by 400 is a leap year | 2000 was a leap year, 1900 was not |

2028 passes the first rule and the other two do not apply to it, because it is not a century year. That is the whole answer: 2028 is a leap year because 4 goes into it exactly.

## February 29 birthdays in 2028

People born on February 29 are called leaplings, and 2028 gives them a real birthday for the first time since 2024. A person born on February 29, 2024 turns 4 on February 29, 2028, even though four calendar years have passed. Someone born on February 29, 2000 turns 28 that day.

In non-leap years the birthday is observed on a nearby date, and the legal date varies by jurisdiction, so check local rules for official documents. The common conventions are February 28 or March 1. Either way, the year count stays correct: age increases every year, not every four years. ## How to check any year

You do not need a list. Test any year in three steps:

1. Divide the year by 4. If there is a remainder, it is not a leap year.
2. If the year ends in 00, divide it by 100. If it is a century year, it is not a leap year.
3. If the century year is also divisible by 400, it is a leap year after all.

Try it: 2032 divided by 4 is 508, so 2032 is a leap year. 2100 divided by 4 is 525, but 2100 is a century year and 2100 divided by 400 is 5.25, so 2100 is not a leap year. 2400 divided by 400 is 6, so 2400 is a leap year.

## Why 2028 matters for planning

Leap years change more than calendars. February 2028 has 29 days, so monthly schedules, billing cycles, and deadlines that fall on February 29 only exist in that year. Contracts and subscriptions that run from March 1, 2027 to March 1, 2028 span 366 days because they include February 29, 2028, while the same span a year later is 365 days.
