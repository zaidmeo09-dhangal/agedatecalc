A year has **250 work days in the US in 2026**: 261 weekdays minus 11 federal holidays. Work days and business days mean the same thing in this post: a weekday that is not a federal holiday. The rest of this post shows the working, the year and month tables, and the PTO and hours math. ## Why 260 is the wrong answer

The number 260 appears on page after page about work days, and it is wrong for the US in 2026. It comes from a rough estimate: 52 weeks times 5 weekdays equals 260. That estimate forgets two things: 2026 has 261 weekdays, not 260, because a 365 day year is 52 weeks plus one extra day, and in 2026 that extra day is Thursday, January 1; and the 260 figure never removes any holidays, even though 11 federal holidays fall inside the year.

The honest arithmetic is 261 minus 11 equals 250. Any page that gives 260 is quoting the weekday estimate before holidays are removed, and it is 10 days too high.

## The formula

Work days equal calendar days minus weekend days minus holidays minus time off:

- Start with 365 calendar days, or 366 in a leap year.
- Remove weekend days: 104 Saturdays and Sundays in a normal year.
- Remove the 11 federal holidays, counting each one on the weekday it is observed.
- Remove your own paid time off to get your personal work days.

For 2026: 365 minus 104 weekend days equals 261 weekdays, then 261 minus 11 federal holidays equals 250 work days. The federal holiday list comes from the [Office of Personnel Management](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/federal-holidays/). Its observed-day rule is what moves the count: a holiday on a Saturday is observed on the Friday before it, and a holiday on a Sunday is observed on the Monday after it. So Independence Day 2026 is observed on Friday, July 3, Christmas 2027 on Friday, December 24, and New Year 2028 on Friday, December 31, 2027, which is why 2027 loses an extra day.

The extra day is December 31, 2027, the observed day for New Year 2028, which falls on a Saturday. In 2028, the count stays at 250 even though it is a leap year: both extra days fall on the weekend, so the weekday count drops to 260, and only 10 holidays are observed inside the year.

## PTO math

Your own work days are 250 minus the paid time off you take:

| PTO days taken | Your work days in 2026 |
|---|---|
| 0 | 250 |
| 5 | 245 |
| 10 | 240 |
| 15 | 235 |
| 20 | 230 |

The average US worker gets 11 vacation days after one year of service, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics National Compensation Survey. At that average, the typical worker actually works 239 days in 2026: 250 minus 11. The same subtraction works for any year: a worker who takes 20 days of PTO in 2027 works 229 days, because 2027 starts at 249, not 250.

## Work hours in a year: 2,080 or 2,000?

Payroll uses 2,080 hours: 52 weeks times 40 hours. The calendar says 2,000 hours: 250 work days times 8 hours. The gap is 80 hours, or 10 work days: payroll counts 260 weekdays, 52 weeks times 5, while the calendar has 261 weekdays minus 11 holidays, which is 250. Dividing a salary by 2,000 instead of 2,080 overstates the hourly rate by 4 percent. For 2027 the calendar figure is 1,992 hours, and for 2028 it is 2,000 hours again.

## Part-time and 4-day weeks

A 4-day week in 2026 gives about 200 work days, but the exact figure depends on which weekday is the day off: 203 days with Monday off, 198 with Tuesday off, 199 with Wednesday or Thursday off, and 201 with Friday off. The day off matters because holidays are not spread evenly across weekdays: Friday holds three of the 2026 holidays in their observed form, so a Friday off wastes fewer work days than a Tuesday off. The range is only 5 days, from 198 to 203, so use 200 as the planning figure and the exact number only when the contract needs it.

## FAQ

**How many work days are in a year?**

250 work days in the US in 2026: 261 weekdays minus 11 federal holidays. 2027 has 249 work days, and 2028 has 250.

**How many weekdays are in a year?**

**How many business days are in a year?**

**Does every company observe all 11 federal holidays?**

No. Federal holidays are days off for federal offices. Private employers choose their own holiday list, so your own count can differ. Use 250 as the standard US baseline, then adjust for your employer. The UK figure is different, about 253 days.
