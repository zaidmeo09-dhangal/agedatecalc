## How this calculator works

Pick a start date, enter a number, choose a unit, and pick a direction. From now counts forward, ago counts back. The start date defaults to today, so the quick picks answer the exact question in one tap.

- **Days:** plain calendar days added or subtracted, one at a time.
- **Weeks:** 7 days per week, so 2 weeks is 14 days.
- **Months:** calendar months, with the day clamped to the last day of the target month when the month is shorter. 4 months from October 31 is February 28, not March 3.
- **Years:** 12 calendar months at a time. February 29, 2028 plus 1 year is February 28, 2029, because 2029 is not a leap year.

## Month ends and leap years

Adding months is the only case where the answer needs a rule. Months have different lengths, so adding months to the 31st can land on a date that does not exist. This calculator clamps to the last day of the target month: 4 months from October 31, 2026 is February 28, 2027, and January 31 plus 1 month is February 28 in a common year, or February 29 in a leap year.

Leap years work the same way. February 29, 2028 plus 1 year is February 28, 2029, because 2029 has no February 29. Adding 4 years from February 29, 2028 lands exactly on February 29, 2032. Not sure if a year is a leap year? Check it with the [Leap Year Calculator]({{ '/leap-year-calculator/' | relative_url }}).

## Days from today in weeks and months

Days, weeks, and months do not convert evenly, so it helps to know the exact split:

| Days | In weeks |
|---|---|
| 7 days | 1 week |
| 10 days | 1 week and 3 days |
| 14 days | 2 weeks |
| 20 days | 2 weeks and 6 days |
| 21 days | 3 weeks |
| 28 days | 4 weeks |
| 30 days | 4 weeks and 2 days |
| 45 days | 6 weeks and 3 days |
| 50 days | 7 weeks and 1 day |
| 60 days | 8 weeks and 4 days |
| 90 days | 12 weeks and 6 days |
| 100 days | 14 weeks and 2 days |
| 120 days | 17 weeks and 1 day |
| 150 days | 21 weeks and 3 days |
| 180 days | 25 weeks and 5 days |
| 365 days | 52 weeks and 1 day |

Months are different, because months have 28 to 31 days. 30 days is about one month, 90 days is about three months, and 365 days is 12 months, but "about" is not exact. If you need a calendar month, use the months unit above instead of guessing 30 days: January 31 plus 1 month is February 28, 2027, because February is shorter and the date clamps to the last day of the month, while January 31 plus 30 days is March 2, 2027. To measure the gap between two fixed dates, use the [Date Difference Calculator]({{ '/date-difference-calculator/' | relative_url }}).

## How to add days to a date in Excel

Put the start date in cell A1 and add the number of days:

`=A1+30`

That gives the date 30 days after the date in A1. For the count from today, use TODAY() instead of a fixed date:

`=TODAY()+30`

The same formulas work in Google Sheets. Change 30 to any number: =TODAY()+7 gives 7 days from today, and =TODAY()-30 gives 30 days ago. Format the result cell as a date if it shows a number instead. To add calendar months rather than days, use =EDATE(A1,3), which adds 3 months to the date in A1 and clamps to the last day of the month when needed. For counting only workdays, use the [Business Days Calculator]({{ '/business-days-calculator/' | relative_url }}).
